Child protection

Online safety

Digital spaces can be useful, creative and social. They also need boundaries, privacy and support.

Protect passwords, think before sharing personal information, use privacy settings, block or report harmful behavior and talk to a trusted adult if someone pressures, threatens or tries to isolate you. This is educational information, not a substitute for verified support.

Keep the conversation practical

Use examples that respect dignity and do not show graphic violence or traumatic imagery. Avoid asking children to share more personal information than is needed. Make it clear when a resource is a draft, when a contact has not been verified and what a person can expect next.

Teach body autonomy, boundaries, trusted adults and the right to say no in age-appropriate language. FIKER should review this content with safeguarding professionals and local partners.
Name what bullying can look like, encourage safe documentation and make it clear that the person experiencing harm is not to blame.
Provide a clear path to an approved safeguarding contact. Do not publish unverified helplines or promise absolute confidentiality.
Create practical conversation guides, signs to pay attention to, referral steps and safe documentation after expert review.
